# First law audit, 2026-10-17

This audit judges each law of `CONSTITUTION.md` at `f97212e` against outcomes **outside** the constitution: reverts,
red CI on main, gate failures on pull-request branches, review findings recorded on pull requests, pull requests closed
unmerged with a law cited (Ryan's corrections), and suppressions left standing in consumer repositories. It does not
judge a law by its own instruments' green runs.

Every number below was computed by code, from data a tool produced. Where a number needs a model's judgment, the
judgment is a Jev typed decision (`typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917` through `@systemfsoftware/discern`) with an uncertain
branch, and the report says how far to trust it. The recommendations are mine, made from the numbers. They are
proposals: nothing in `CONSTITUTION.md` or `ENFORCEMENT.md` changes in this pull request.

## What the audit found first: most laws have no instrument it can count

`ENFORCEMENT.md` binds each law to rungs (`type`, `command`, `review`) and, for some, to a `command:` phrase ("lint",
"import-graph lint", "mutation gate, break 100"). It never names a lint rule id, a CI check name or a review-agent rule.
Searching the monorepo's lint plugins finds exactly one rule that names a law (`oxlint-plugin-test-discipline`'s `lane`,
CONST-T12). `systemf-review-agent`'s 21 `SRA-*` rules name no law at all.

So a "prevented" count can be attributed mechanically for only two laws:

- **CONST-T3** — the mutation gate is named by T3 alone.
- **CONST-D1** — the type rung is named by D1 (B1 names it only as a technique). This is an upper bound, since `tsc`
  fails on any type error, not only an unclosed type.

Seven laws (D1, D2, D4, B1, B4, N1, T12) claim "command: lint". Lint failures therefore form one pool that no single
law can claim. The pool's logs do not help: 0 of 303 lint and static-check job logs printed a rule id, because the lane
logs point to a failure artifact, and 35 of them failed because the linter could not load its own config. The other
eight laws are review-only. On GitHub their review findings live in pull-request bodies, review summaries and
comments, not in review states. Across 1,938 public pull requests there are 0 `CHANGES_REQUESTED` reviews and 7
inline threads, none citing a law.

**Recommendation (applies to every law): each corpus entry should name its instruments by id.** That means lint rule
ids, CI check names, and `SRA-*` rule ids. Until it does, "prevented" stays a judgment rather than a count, and
CONST-S7's test ("a law that names no prevented incident is a retirement candidate") cannot be applied mechanically.

## Per-law notes

**CONST-D1 CLOSED-TYPES: amend.**
- **Prevented.**
  - The type rung failed 17 PRs, and 6 of them logged real `TSxxxx` errors. Most of those 6 are Dependabot majors
    caught by `tsc`, which is real value but not specifically a closed-type value.
  - Review caught 2 PRs: gritlint#20, where open-string reason codes became tagged, and systemfsoftware#747.
- **Recurrence.** 4 incidents (2 since the rewrite).
- **Harm check.** Uncertain on both cited incidents. The lock-adapter incident's harm, running unlocked, is not visible
  in ±3 lines.
- **Cost.** 167 TypeScript and cast suppressions stand in consumer code outside xstate. The audit does not split
  legitimate type-test `@ts-expect-error` from waivers beyond test paths: of the 915, 84 sit outside test paths.
- **Recommendation.** Keep the law. Amend the corpus entry to name its instrument ids. The suppressions need an owner's
  triage, because ENFORCEMENT's enrolment rule ("enroll over a clean tree only") is not met.

**CONST-D2 ERROR-VARIANTS: keep; change rung.**
- **Harm check.** Shown on both corpus incidents. This is the strongest harm signal in the corpus, though one control
  was also "shown" on the same incident.
- **Prevented.** One caught violation: stryker-js-effect#286.
- **Rung.** Its "command: lint" names no rule the audit could find, so the binding rung in practice is review.
- **Recommendation.** Record the rung as review, or build and name the lint.

**CONST-D4 STATES-AS-UNIONS: keep; change rung.**
- **Prevented.** One caught violation: systemfsoftware#736, where a pin-site union was told apart by `typeof`.
- **Recurrence.** 2 incidents, both before the rewrite.
- **Rung.** Its criteria describe a lint that "flags an optional that correlates with the discriminant". No such rule
  id was found.

**CONST-B1 EFFECTS-AT-EDGES: amend.**
- **Instruments.** It names three lints: no eager async, decisions import no I/O, and cyclomatic complexity 1. None is
  named by id.
- **Prevented.** One caught violation, on an open PR: gritlint#24.
- **Recurrence.** No ledger incident.
- **Absence of evidence.** By CONST-S7, B1 names little prevented harm. That absence is a data gap, not evidence: the
  lint pool cannot be split. Re-audit once the instruments are named.

**CONST-B4 DEPENDENCIES-INWARD: amend.** Harm shown on one of its two incidents. One caught violation (gritlint#20,
where the core opened stdio). No recurrence. Name the import-graph rule. The same data gap as B1 applies.

**CONST-N1 ORGANISED-BY-PURPOSE: retirement candidate, with a data-gap caveat.**
- **Prevented.** One citation was read as caught (stryker-js-effect#296), and reading it, it looks closer to a
  mention.
- **Recurrence and harm.** No recurrence, and harm not shown.
- **Data gap.** Its filename lint, if enrolled, is in the unsplittable lint pool, so the absence may be a gap.
- **Recommendation.** Ryan decides: retire it, or name the filename lint and re-audit.

**CONST-N3 FITS-IN-THE-HEAD: retirement candidate.** It is review-only, with 0 caught violations, 0 recurrence, and
harm not shown on either incident. CONST-S7 applies directly. The caveat is that review-rung findings made in agent
sessions never reach GitHub, so this is "no evidence found", not "evidence of no harm".

**CONST-S1 ROOT-CAUSE: change rung.**
- **Recurrence.** 8 ledger incidents (6 red CI on main, 2 reverts), all since 2026-10-09: the most of any law in that
  window.
- **Prevented.** One weak review catch.
- **Weighting.** At TPR 0.47 the true number is likely higher. The classifier may also prefer S1 for any fix-shaped
  incident: S1 was not correctly classified in held-out.
- **Recommendation.** A review-only law that recurs this often is under-enforced. The candidate instrument is outside
  the constitution and already exists: a revert or red-main incident from the ledger routes to a root-cause review
  (fleet-graph step 3).

**CONST-S2 FIRST-PRINCIPLES: retirement candidate.** 0 caught violations, 1 incident before the rewrite, and harm not
shown on either incident (a package name, and a copied gate command). CONST-S7 applies. Part of its content overlaps
N1, a misnaming inherited from a plan.

**CONST-S4 SUBTRACT-FIRST: amend.**
- **Evidence.** 0 caught violations, 1 recurrence, harm not shown, and 4 uncertain citations.
- **Data gap.** As a maker law it shows no counted prevention, but its harm (net growth, rot) is measured by line
  delta, which this audit did not compute.
- **Recommendation.** Keep the subtraction rule as the audit's own operating rule. The goal's "CONST-S7" has no live
  id, and its content is S4 plus ENFORCEMENT's "every gate names the mistake it prevents". Add a net-line-delta
  measure before judging S4 as a maker law.

**CONST-T8 PUBLIC-SURFACE: change rung.**
- **Recurrence.** 6 red-CI incidents (3 since the rewrite) despite the law.
- **Prevented.** One weak review catch.
- **Harm check.** Uncertain on both incidents.
- **Recommendation.** Its own corpus "right" already names a command: "the pack check reads what each tarball
  contains". Bind T8 to that check, and to `attw`, at the command rung.

**CONST-T10 INDEPENDENT-ORACLE: keep.** This is the best-evidenced review-rung law.
- **Prevented.** 4 caught. Three are xstate#54–56, closed unmerged by Ryan with T10 named, which is the outside anchor
  this audit wants.
- **Waivers.** 2, including systemfsoftware#761 "T10 conflict declared until the upstream change is vendored".
- **Recurrence.** 2 incidents.

**CONST-T3 TESTS-CAN-FAIL: keep the law; amend the instrument.**
- **What the instrument cost.** The mutation gate failed 3,305 jobs on 124 PR branches and spent 10,240 CI minutes,
  most of it in August, before mutation left PRs.
- **What was a verdict.** Only 118 jobs on 11 branches stated the instrument's own verdict (a score under break), and
  3 of those 11 branches are Dependabot bumps, carrying 96 of the 118 jobs. The rest were plumbing: 1,581 jobs with no mutation report, about
  1,566 crashes or unstated failures, and 47 test-contribution refusals.
- **Prevented.** Against maker work, the gate's own verdict caught 8 branches.
- **Escape.** stryker-js-effect#327 records merged code that violates T3.
- **Recurrence.** 8 incidents (3 since the rewrite).
- **Harm.** Shown on both incidents.
- **Suppressions.** 32 Stryker suppressions stand (30 in stryker-js-effect), which T3's own criteria forbid.
- **Recommendation.** The law holds up. The instrument's cost is overwhelmingly its own failure, not blocked correct
  work. `SRA-MUTATION-PLACEMENT` already moved mutation to the release gate, leaving 18 PR jobs in October. Clear or
  waive-by-declaration the 32 suppressions.

**CONST-T12 TEST-OBLIGATION: keep; strengthen the rung.**
- **Recurrence.** 11 ledger incidents (3 since the rewrite), the most of any law overall.
- **Prevented.** 3 caught, 2 of them closed: systemfsoftware#675 and constitution#27.
- **Harm.** Shown on the path-label incident.
- **Instrument.** Only one lint rule (`lane`) names it.
- **Recommendation.** Recurrence despite a command rung means the lint covers too little. Name and extend the
  instruments, for example the mutation-target selection the corpus's own fix describes.

**CONST-W1 FULL-SCOPE: keep pending data.**
- **Evidence.** 0 counted catches, 0 recurrence, and harm not shown: the evidence is two plan documents.
- **Data gap.** W1's harm shows in Ryan's corrections ("the requester refused") in sessions, Linear and chat, which
  this audit did not mine.
- **Recommendation.** Mining those corrections is the next data source. Until then, absence here is a gap, not
  evidence.

**CONST-E7 EVIDENCE-NOT-SELF-GRADING: amend.**
- **Waivers.** 6 PRs: the most of any law, mostly declared under the absorbed CONST-E9. Examples are
  constitution#30, systemfsoftware#489, #562 and #747, and stryker-js-effect#271 and #303.
- **Prevented.** One catch, closed (systemfsoftware#705).
- **Harm.** Not shown on 3 of its 4 incidents, which are review-finding docs.
- **Recommendation.** The law joins two obligations (show evidence; never touch the grader). The waivers show the
  second is routinely declared around. Tighten what a valid E7 declaration is, and bind "never edit the grader" to the
  existing refusal rung (Symphony's frozen nodes, `SRA-THRESHOLD-LOOSEN`).

## Limits

- **Review rung (uncalibrated).** The citation reading is uncalibrated, and some `violation-caught` readings look like
  mentions (N1's stryker-js-effect#296, S1's #748, T8's xstate#75). The appendix lists every one.
- **Harm check (uncalibrated).** It sees only ±3 lines and the fix's hunks. "Not shown" for a doc or plan citation
  mostly reflects thin evidence.
- **Recurrence (advisory classifier).** TPR 0.47; 79 of the 128 incidents went to `ungated-principle`.
- **Suppressions.** The xstate tree is upstream code; it is shown separately above.
- **Not mined.** Ryan's corrections in sessions and Linear, maker time, and review-agent verdicts outside GitHub.
